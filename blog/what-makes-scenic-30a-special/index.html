What Makes Scenic 30A Special?

Scenic Highway 30A is more than a road connecting communities along the Gulf. It is a corridor shaped by an unusual combination of natural beauty, distinct communities, state lands, trails, coastal dune lakes, history and recreation.

That combination is what led to Scenic 30A's recognition as a Florida Scenic Highway—and later as a National Scenic Byway. It is also what Friends of Scenic 30A works to protect.

A Collection of Distinct Communities

One of the things that makes Scenic 30A unusual is that it isn't defined by a single town.

The corridor passes through a collection of communities, each with its own history, architecture and character. From long-established communities such as Grayton Beach, Seagrove and Santa Rosa Beach to newer planned communities such as Seaside, WaterColor, Alys Beach and Rosemary Beach, the experience changes as you travel along 30A.

Friends' historical materials emphasized these individual communities as an important part of the Scenic 30A experience.

Preserving 30A therefore means more than preserving a view from the roadway. It means respecting the individuality and character of the communities through which the road passes.

An Extraordinary Natural Environment

Scenic 30A also passes through some of Northwest Florida's most significant natural areas.

State parks, Point Washington State Forest, trails, wetlands, beaches and other protected lands create a landscape where developed communities and natural areas exist remarkably close together.

But perhaps no natural feature is more closely associated with Scenic 30A than its coastal dune lakes .

These unusual lakes sit just behind the dunes and periodically exchange water with the Gulf of Mexico through natural outlets. Friends has long recognized the coastal dune lakes as among the corridor's most important natural resources.

Eastern Lake, Western Lake, Deer Lake, Camp Creek Lake, Draper Lake and others are not simply scenic backdrops. They are part of what makes this area environmentally distinctive.

A Corridor Meant to Be Experienced

Scenic 30A isn't only something people experience through a windshield.

Walking, bicycling and exploring the corridor have long been part of its identity.

The Timpoochee Trail connects communities and provides residents and visitors with another way to experience 30A. State parks and Point Washington State Forest provide additional opportunities for hiking, bicycling and experiencing the area's natural landscape.

That is one reason bicycle and pedestrian safety, trail maintenance and connectivity have been longstanding priorities for Friends of Scenic 30A.

More Than a Scenic Road

The Florida Scenic Highway program recognizes roads not simply because they are beautiful, but because the corridors surrounding them possess important scenic, natural, historic, cultural, archaeological and recreational resources.

Scenic 30A has all of them.

Protecting those resources while accommodating the needs of the people who live, work and visit here requires thoughtful planning and continued community involvement.

That's the role Friends of Scenic 30A continues to play.

Our mission is not to freeze 30A in time. It is to help ensure that as the area changes, the qualities that made Scenic 30A worth protecting in the first place aren't lost along the way.
